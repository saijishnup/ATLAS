Prototyping builds a quick, partial version of the product to explore unclear requirements or test a risky idea before committing to full development. Stakeholders try the prototype, give feedback, and the prototype is refined or thrown away. It fits projects where users cannot describe what they need but can react to something concrete, such as a new in-car interface concept or a driver-monitoring screen layout. It fails when the prototype is mistaken for the final product, which carries poor structure and weak testing into production, and when stakeholders are unavailable for review. Typical roles: designer, developers, user representatives, product owner. Deliverables: a prototype, feedback notes, a refined requirement list. Usually paired with another model for the real build, not used alone for safety-relevant software.
