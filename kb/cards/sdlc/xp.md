Extreme Programming relies on engineering practices: pair programming, test-driven development, continuous integration, refactoring, small releases, and an on-site customer. Short cycles keep feedback fast and design simple. It fits small teams building software with unclear or changing requirements and few external approvals, such as a prototype diagnostics client. It fails when a regulator or standard demands upfront documentation and independent verification, or when the team is large or distributed. Typical roles: developers, customer representative, coach. Deliverables: working code, automated tests, and frequent releases. In automotive its practices, especially automated testing and continuous integration, are often borrowed inside other models rather than used alone.
